Diffusion Monte Carlo

Pure diffusion Monte Carlo propagates walkers in imaginary time. For $H=-D\nabla^2+V$, one step moves every walker by a Gaussian displacement of variance $2D\Delta t$ from $\pmb{r}$ to $\pmb{r}'$ and assigns the branching weight

\[w = \exp\left[-\Delta t\left(\frac{V(\pmb{r})+V(\pmb{r}')}{2}-E_\mathrm{ref}\right)\right],\]

with the exponent capped at log_weight_cap so that a walker near a singularity of the potential cannot take over the population. DiffusionMonteCarlo keeps the population fixed by systematic resampling and estimates the ground-state energy as the average of the growth estimator $E_\mathrm{ref}-\log\langle w\rangle/\Delta t$ after equilibration. A seeded random-number generator makes a calculation reproducible within one Julia version.

Usage

using TwoBody

H = Hamiltonian(
  Kinetic(hbar=1, m=1),
  PowerLaw(coefficient=1 / 2, exponent=2),
)
method = DiffusionMonteCarlo(
  n_steps=800,
  equilibration=200,
  n_walkers=1_000,
  Δt=0.01,
)
result = solve(H, method)
result.E
1.507439703829858

Finite time steps and walker populations introduce bias. Convergence should be checked by reducing $\Delta t$ and increasing n_walkers. See Reynolds et al. (1990) and Kosztin et al. (1996). The reported standard_error is estimated from block averages and still underestimates the spread between independent runs by a factor of about three at the default settings; compare several seeds for a reliable error. No importance sampling is used, so the Coulomb potential carries a bias of a few percent at these settings and relies on the cap of the branching weight.

TwoBody.solve — Method

solve(hamiltonian, method::DiffusionMonteCarlo; rng=Random.MersenneTwister(123), initial=nothing)

Run the calculation and return a ResultDiffusionMonteCarlo. rng seeds the walk, which is reproducible within one Julia version. initial is an optional $3\times n_\mathrm{walkers}$ matrix of starting positions. The Hamiltonian must contain a nonrelativistic kinetic term (Kinetic or Laplacian), may contain RestEnergy and potential terms with a finite V(o, r); Delta, Tabulated, and the relativistic kinetic terms are rejected.
